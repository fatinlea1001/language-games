The Last Promise

We promised to meet on that day but neither of us knew that our future together would disappear before it began. Sara and I had known each other since Form One, yet it had taken us years to admit that our friendship was becoming something more. We agreed to meet at our school's Saturday , where I planned to tell her how I truly felt. I counted down the days as though I were waiting for the most important moment of my life.

On Friday afternoon, Sara stopped beside my classroom. "Promise me you won't keep me waiting," she said, smiling. I promised, although my hands were shaking with excitement. That evening, I bought her favourite white daisies and wrote a letter. I had always been to express my feelings, but this time I wanted to be brave. Every time I pictured her reading the letter, excitement rushed through me.

I reached the carnival before sunset. Colourful lanterns hung above the stalls, music drifted across the field and the air smelled of grilled food. I checked my watch , imagining the smile on Sara's face when she saw me. However, dark clouds began gathering overhead. I worried that the storm might our meeting, but I refused to leave. Every time someone walked through the entrance, my heart leapt with excitement. None of them was Sara.

Suddenly, her brother's name appeared on my phone. "Sara was hit by a car on her way there. I'm so sorry," he whispered. For a moment, I could not move. My father drove me to the hospital, but the doctors had already done everything they could. The news was . I stared at the flowers in my hands, unable to accept that she would never take them. All the happiness I had felt just an hour earlier disappeared.

A week later, I found a small inside my school locker. Sara had slipped it there before the carnival. Inside was a photograph of us at our first school sports day and a note saying she had been waiting for the courage to tell me she loved me. I sat alone on the empty school field, holding both letters against my chest. We had finally found the same words, only to lose the chance to say them. Some promises, I realised, remain forever unfulfilled.
