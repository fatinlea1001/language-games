The Day I Began Again

I stared at my examination results and felt as if the world had suddenly become silent.... A red 38 was printed beside Mathematics. Around me, my classmates were celebrating their good results. I had studied for weeks, yet I had failed. Feeling disappointed, I quickly hid the paper inside my school bag.

At home, I when Mother asked about my results. "I failed Maths," I whispered. I expected her to scold me, but she sat beside me instead. "One result does not decide your future," she said gently. Her words made me cry because I had expected her to be angry.

The next day, I met my Mathematics teacher, Mr Hafiz. He explained that this did not mean I was a bad student. Together, we checked my mistakes and discovered that I often skipped important steps. He showed me how to solve each question carefully. From then on, I practised five questions every evening.

Two weeks later, my younger brother Amir came home crying because he had failed a spelling test. "I'm stupid," he said sadly. I tried to him, but I remembered saying the same thing about myself. I sat beside him and helped him practise his spelling. Slowly, he smiled and agreed to try again.

A month later, I scored 54 in my Mathematics test. I felt and showed Amir my result. "See? We can both improve!" I told him happily. That evening, I placed my old result beside the new one. The number 38 was still there, but it no longer made me feel ashamed. It reminded me that failing once did not mean I had to give up forever.
